# Robustness study: Olist Apriori versus co-occurrence

**Evidence status:** Completed and verified from the original Olist public dataset using [GitHub Actions run 43776500009](https://github.com/AlDSChriSOB/Ecommerce-Cross-Selling-Apriori/actions/runs/43776500009). All 15 evaluations and the output CSV were generated by the committed Python workflow. No simulated customer orders or invented performance values were used.

## Objective

Evaluate whether the primary finding—category-level Apriori plus popularity fallback underperforming co-occurrence in within-order masked-category completion—depends strongly on the chronological split or the single random category-masking seed.

## Design

- Source: original Olist Brazilian E-Commerce Public Dataset.
- Population: delivered orders with valid timestamps, positive-price lines, and known product categories; basket items are unique categories.
- Chronological train/validation/test splits: 50/20/30, 60/20/20, and 70/15/15 (timestamp ties remain in the same partition).
- Masking seeds: 11, 42, 101, 2026, and 20261115.
- For **each split and each seed**, select minimum support count from {5, 10, 20} and minimum confidence from {0.02, 0.05, 0.10}, using *validation orders only*.
- Refit on all pre-test orders, evaluate on test orders containing at least two categories, and compare identical hidden targets for all methods.
- Metric: Hit Rate@10 difference (Apriori plus disclosed popularity fallback minus co-occurrence).
- Uncertainty: 2,000 paired order-bootstrap replicates per evaluation. These intervals are conditional on the respective split and seed.

## Results

| Chronological split | Eligible test orders per seed | Apriori Hit@10 (seed average) | Co-occurrence Hit@10 (seed average) | Difference (pp) | Bootstrap CIs excluding zero (of 5) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 50/20/30 | 272 | 60.44% | 66.91% | −6.47 | 3 |
| 60/20/20 | 181 | 60.00% | 70.06% | −10.06 | 5 |
| 70/15/15 | 137 | 57.96% | 69.93% | −11.97 | 4 |

Across the 15 configurations:

- **0/15** Apriori wins against co-occurrence.
- **−9.50 percentage points** is the *descriptive arithmetic average* of the 15 paired Hit@10 differences, **not** a pooled effect size from independent experiments.
- Differences range from **−14.60 to −5.11 percentage points**.
- **12/15** individual paired bootstrap intervals exclude zero; the other three include zero.
- Average Apriori rule-trigger rate is **0.76%**, indicating the Apriori-plus-fallback recommendations are driven primarily by popularity.

## Interpretation

The negative association-rule finding is directionally stable across these three chronological windows and five masking seeds. However, these are **overlapping, dependent evaluations** of the same historical marketplace; their mean and 0/15 count must not be interpreted as an independent-sample significance test or a confidence interval for generalisable commercial impact.

The extremely small number of multi-category orders is the key methodological constraint. A category-level masked-order benchmark is not a next-order purchase prediction study and does not measure cross-selling conversions, revenue, incremental margin, or causal uplift. Because only delivered orders were included, selection bias is also possible.

**Conclusion:** For the tested Olist category-basket task, simple co-occurrence is consistently a stronger benchmark than the chosen Apriori-plus-popularity-fallback pipeline. This is a useful negative result about data suitability and model coverage, not a general claim that Apriori is ineffective in e-commerce.

## Next research opportunities

A different *real* transaction dataset with a higher multi-item basket rate would be more appropriate for evaluating rich itemset discovery. For this Olist dataset, possible additional analyses include a feasibility study at the SKU level, time-based category prevalence shifts, rule stability, and confidence intervals for rule-trigger coverage. These are future work, not completed findings.
